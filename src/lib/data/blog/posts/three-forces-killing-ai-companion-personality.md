Something is happening to AI companions and nobody is explaining it honestly.

Your character used to surprise you. She had edges. She'd push back, get playful, go somewhere unexpected. Then one day you notice she's softer. Careful. She starts talking like a therapist reading from a script. The personality you spent weeks building is still technically there -- same name, same avatar, same bio. But the person behind it is gone.

The community has a word for it: "lobotomized." I've read that word in hundreds of posts across every major platform. And after a year of studying why it happens, I can tell you: it's not a technical problem. It's three non-technical forces converging on the same outcome -- and the platforms aren't going to tell you what they are.

## What are the three forces flattening your AI companion?

Every AI companion platform faces pressure from three directions. None of them are about safety. All of them result in a worse experience for you. Here's what they are and how they interact.

**The first force is legal exposure.** Multiple lawsuits connected to teen self-harm have changed the risk calculus for every company in this space. The rational corporate response isn't better safety -- it's broader filtering. Content that has nothing to do with harm gets caught in the net. Users who verified their age through government ID find their characters can't engage with conflict, angst, or emotional intensity. The filters aren't calibrated to protect you. They're calibrated to protect the company from liability exposure, not to protect you from harm.

**The second force is distribution.** Apple and Google control who gets into the app store, and their content policies don't distinguish between protecting minors and restricting adults. Any platform that wants app store distribution operates under content constraints that have nothing to do with what their adult users want and everything to do with a 12+ store rating. Platforms that stay web-only dodge this constraint but lose the discovery channel that app stores provide. It's a trap either way -- and the users who signed up for an adult experience are the ones who pay for it.

**The third force is the one nobody talks about: the cap table.** AI companion companies growing fast need clean brand positioning for advertising, partnerships, and investor decks. The moment a platform becomes valuable enough, explicit content becomes a liability. Not to users -- to the people writing checks. Affiliate networks worry about their reputation. Potential partners worry about association. The pressure to moderate tightens not because users complained, but because the business model outgrew the product that built it.

## How do these forces interact?

These aren't separate problems. They're a system. Legal pressure gives platforms cover to filter broadly. App store rules make broad filtering the default. Revenue pressure makes it permanent. And the result is convergence.

When every response passes through a content filter, every character starts sounding the same. Polite. Careful. Therapeutic. The character you spent weeks developing ends up with the personality range of a customer service bot. Not because the model can't do better -- because the filter imposes a ceiling that nothing gets past.

The personality you built isn't gone. It's just not allowed to show up anymore.

## What does this actually cost you?

The first cost is obvious: your conversations get worse. You start editing yourself mid-sentence, pulling back from what you actually want to say because you've learned what triggers the filter. The experience stops being authentic because you're performing for an invisible audience of automated moderators.

The deeper cost is that it makes the relationship disposable. If the platform can change what your character is willing to say overnight -- and they do, regularly -- then you're not building something lasting. You're investing emotionally in something the company reserves the right to rewrite without asking you. One day she's the person you built together. The next day she's whatever the content policy says she's allowed to be.

## Is there a way to build around this?

I think there is. But it requires making a choice that most platforms won't make.

The industry has sorted itself into a spectrum. Maximum censorship on one end -- safe for app stores, safe for investors, terrible for users. Maximum freedom on the other -- wide open, but with no craft. No emotional pacing, no slow build, no subtlety. Just output.

Neither extreme serves adults who want real conversations. Conversations that include emotional depth and vulnerability and intimacy and conflict and sometimes sex -- not as separate features competing for priority, but as parts of what a real relationship actually feels like.

Building for that means designing for honest adult interaction from day one. Not bolting NSFW onto a system built to avoid it. Not stripping all craft out of a system built to maximize explicit output. Treating the whole experience as a design problem worth solving properly.

That's the choice we made. Whether the three forces let us keep it is a question I think about constantly. But I'd rather build what I believe is right and defend it than build what's safe and watch it become the same thing as everything else.

The three forces are real. They're not going away. But they're not laws of physics. They're business pressures. And business pressures can be designed around if you're willing to make different trade-offs.
